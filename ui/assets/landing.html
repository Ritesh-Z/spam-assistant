FOUR STEPS, one SECOND

What happens to every email between your inbox and its label.

  1. 01

    Read

    Signs in with Google and fetches your latest 50 emails. Read-only, and nothing is stored.

    gmail.readonly
  2. 02

    Clean

    Strips HTML and headers, turns links, addresses and numbers into tokens, then builds TF-IDF over words and word pairs.

    tf-idf · 1–2 grams
  3. 03

    Score

    Gmail's sender verification comes first. Then a Linear SVM on words and character patterns scores the text; when it is unsure, a DistilBERT model gives a second opinion.

    sender check + ml
  4. 04

    Explain

    You see the label, the trigger words and the warning signs. Wrong? One click teaches the model.

    feedback → retrain

FIVE colours, ONE GLANCE

A high bar for Spam keeps real mail visible. Hiding a real email costs more than missing a spam. Mail you signed up for is split into marketing and updates, so it never drowns the rest.

Spam

Spam probability of 85% or more. Phishing, scams, junk.

≥ 85%

Suspicious

Between 60% and 85%. Not hidden, but worth a careful look.

60 – 85%

Promotion

Marketing you signed up for: Gmail's Promotions tab, or sales wording like "30% off" or "try Pro".

list mail · ads

Update

Automated notices: account and security notices, terms changes, product news, newsletters.

list mail · notices

Safe

Everything else: people, colleagues, personal mail.

< 60%
⚑ ALERT

A flag, not a label. Security notices, sign-in codes and payment problems from a sender Gmail verified as a known service or bank are flagged and pinned to the top of your inbox. Urgent wording from anyone else is a warning sign instead, so phishing can never earn the flag.
